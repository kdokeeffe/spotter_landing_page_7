A concept for volunteer-run youth gymnastics clubs in Ireland

Find out what your coaches can do, and how to get them ready for the next level.

The Spotter Sheet concept keeps each coach’s safeguarding, vetting status, first aid and coach tier by date, warns before anything lapses, and shows every teenage gymnast-coach their next step. Parents and children can use the same tool to follow progress and ask for the next challenge.

Who is our solution aimed at?

The segment
The club this concept imagines: a small, volunteer-run gymnastics club in Ireland with several age groups, from about 4 years old up to competitive teenagers, where some coaches are teenage gymnasts who coach while still training themselves. This is the assumed starting point, not a survey of real clubs.
Who decides and pays
Assumed to be the club committee, for example the chair or the treasurer.
Who uses it every day
  • Parents & children can track the child’s progress.
  • Gymnast coaches see their own profile and group, and can make updates and process changes.
  • The club admin (for example the head coach or the children’s officer) keeps the records.

What a solution must do

Six short requirements. You can look for each one in the demo.

  1. Show each coach’s safeguarding, vetting status, first aid and coach tier, with dates, on one screen.
  2. Warn automatically from the dates: amber 60 days before a date, red once it has passed (60 days is a sample setting).
  3. Show which groups each coach meets the club’s requirements for, and the next step that would unlock another group.
  4. Let a coach submit or renew a certificate and ask for a training, with the club admin deciding.
  5. Let parents and children follow a child’s progress and ask the club for the next challenge, and let coaches update progress and recommend a group move, with the club admin deciding.
  6. Work on a phone, and keep only names, progress, statuses and dates, never ID documents or vetting disclosures.

How clubs cope today, and what already exists

Assumption, not research: I expect small clubs to keep this in spreadsheets, paper and group chats, with one volunteer chasing people by message. I have not surveyed any club, and I would confirm this with real volunteers before building anything.

Two real tools already do part of the job. The page for each was read on .

ClearCheck Checked

What it does: compliance tracking for Irish sports clubs. A dashboard of who is compliant, expiring or missing, one-click reminders, guided Garda vetting steps, and requirements mapped to roles such as coach or committee member. Its page says it is used by GAA, rugby and soccer clubs.

Not mentioned in the parts of the page that were read: gymnastics, age groups, or a path for a young coach to move up.

Outcoach Checked

What it does: gymnastics club management. Classes and terms, payments, coach ratios, messaging parents, and gymnast skill goals and sign-offs that parents can see.

Not mentioned in the parts of the page that were read: tracking of coaches’ vetting or safeguarding dates.

Each site may say more on pages that were not read. Other tools exist, so Spotter Sheet is not claiming to be the first date tracker. The idea it tests is joining the two halves: coach credentials checked against each age group, plus a visible next step for a teenager who is both a gymnast and a coach.

What Spotter Sheet is

A phone-first web app, part tracker and part approvals portal. It has three views: Child/Parent, Gymnast Coach and Club Admin.

How it works

  1. In a real version, the club would enter each person’s dates and set what each age group needs. The demo has these already set up as invented samples.
  2. The app works out each date’s status, which groups each coach meets the requirements for, and the next step.
  3. Coaches ask for a certificate, a training or a level, and children and parents ask for the next challenge. The coach or the club admin approves or declines.

Why it tracks a status date, not a verdict

Gymnastics Ireland’s vetting policy says anyone whose role has ongoing contact with its young members must complete Garda e-Vetting. Sport Ireland’s guidance says each governing body is responsible for how it runs vetting, and points readers to their own governing body’s procedures for re-vetting. So the demo only records a date and compares dates with invented sample requirements. It is not a vetting or compliance check, and the club stays responsible for who works with children.

Built for the gym floor

These are aims, not measured results: less chasing, fewer surprises, and a clear next step for every coach, kept up to date by busy volunteers on a phone.

  • Quick updates where the work happens. Most things are a tap or two, so records can be updated quickly in the gym.
  • Clear for everyone. Each role gets a view made for its job: a parent follows their child, a coach sees their group, and the club admin sees the whole club.
  • Easy to correct. Most changes can be undone straight away from the confirmation message, and every confirmation has an OK button.
  • No guessing what is real. A sample-data banner stays at the top of every demo screen, so it is clear these are not real records.
  • Made for a small screen. Large buttons and short lines are meant to keep it readable on a phone.

How it would be paid for

Assumption, not tested with any club: an annual club subscription of €240, about €20 a month.

The reasoning is that a volunteer committee can approve a small, fixed cost without a long discussion. The benefit it would have to earn is time saved by the person who keeps the records, and I have not measured that. A sponsor or a club-support grant would be other options to test.
